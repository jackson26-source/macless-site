Codemagic and Bitrise vs Macless

Both are polished, full-service mobile CI platforms that also solve "no Mac." The difference isn't whether they work, it's whether you're paying for it once or every month.

Jackson · August 2026

The short version: Codemagic and Bitrise are real, capable products. You don't need a Mac with either because the build runs entirely in their cloud, and both handle signing for you through their own UI. What you're actually choosing between isn't capability, it's the billing model: a per-minute or monthly plan that continues for as long as you ship the app, versus $19.99/month or $299 once for a pipeline that lives in your own repo and keeps running whether you're paying or not.

Who's actually doing the work Their hosted build infrastructure, behind their own dashboard and config format. Your own GitHub Actions runners, in a plain workflow file you can open, read, and modify.
What you have after it's done A working pipeline tied to their platform, their pricing tier, and their continued willingness to run it. A YAML file in your own repo. No vendor, no seat count, no plan to outgrow.

If you're on a team with heavy build volume, private repos where public-runner minutes don't apply, or you want a managed dashboard and support contract, Codemagic or Bitrise's paid infrastructure is a legitimate, often better choice. This page isn't arguing you should never pay for CI.

If you're a solo developer or small team shipping from a public repo (or fine making it public), the free GitHub Actions minutes that Codemagic and Bitrise also ultimately run on top of are available to you directly. Macless is the one-time cost of wiring that up correctly, instead of paying someone else monthly to have already wired it up for you.

Why this comparison matters

Codemagic and Bitrise aren't the "hard way" and Macless isn't the "easy way" — both solve the actual signing and provisioning problem, which is the part that actually costs time. The real difference shows up in month three, not day one: their bill scales with how much you build, and their builds stop the day you stop paying — a pipeline in your own repo does neither. If you're shipping for years, that gap compounds. If you want someone else's infrastructure and support behind every build, it might be worth paying for regardless.
