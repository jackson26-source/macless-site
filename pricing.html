What you're actually buying

This is the part worth being precise about, because it's the one thing that genuinely differs from every hosted CI service.

The pipeline is a set of files in your own GitHub repository. A workflow file, signing scripts, and docs. When it runs, it runs on GitHub's macOS and Linux runners, billed to your GitHub account — free on public repos, and out of your monthly allowance on private ones. It signs with certificates you generated under your own Apple Developer account, stored as encrypted GitHub secrets in your repo. There is no Macless server between your code and Apple. If macless.dev went dark tomorrow, your builds would still run tonight.

So you own it outright. There's no subscription to cancel. Your builds run exactly the same whether you ever talk to me again or not — same workflow file, same secrets, same green runs.

What the $99 covers: the setup wizard that installs and configures all of that for you, the workflow file and signing scripts, and the guides and walkthrough video that show you how to run it. If you'd rather not run it yourself, that's what the $299 and premium plans are for.

Before you ask

Is there a subscription?

No. The pipeline is a one-time $99. The workflow files, signing scripts, and docs are committed in your repository, your credentials are in your repository's encrypted secrets, and the builds run on your GitHub Actions minutes. None of that is checked against a subscription, and none of it phones home. The free Signing Doctor and Rejection Doctor core checks stay available to everyone.

Is Android included, or is that extra?

Included in the $99. iOS and Android are one product: the same wizard, the same secrets handling, the same signing setup across your iOS certificates and your Android keystore. Android never needed a Mac in the first place; it needed the same signing setup nobody wants to hand-assemble twice. The $299 ship-it-for-me plan covers one platform, iOS or Android.

Is the Apple Developer Program fee included?

No, and to be unambiguous about it: Apple charges $99 per year for the Apple Developer Program, you pay that directly to Apple, and it has nothing to do with Macless. You need it to put an app on the App Store or TestFlight at all, regardless of what tool you build with. Macless removes the Mac requirement, not Apple's fee.

Which plan should I pick?

$99 if you're comfortable in GitHub and a terminal and want to do the setup yourself. $299 if you're a developer, your app already builds and runs, and you just don't know the Apple side. Premium (from $599) if your app came out of an AI or no-code builder and doesn't work yet, or you aren't sure what's wrong with it. If you're not sure which you are, pick premium or just send me the app and ask.

Do the free tools have a catch?

No account, no card, no usage cap, no trial clock. Signing Doctor and Rejection Doctor's core checks are public tools and they stay that way — they're the part of this I'd want to exist even if nobody ever bought anything.

Are there build costs on top of this?

Not from me. The builds run on GitHub Actions under your account: public repositories get unlimited free macOS minutes, and private repositories get a monthly allowance before GitHub bills you per minute past it. Either way that's your bill with GitHub, at GitHub's rates, and Macless doesn't mark it up or sit in the middle of it.

I already bought or subscribed at an older price. Where does that leave me?

Nothing is taken away from you. What you downloaded is in your repo and keeps running, the same as it did the day you bought it. If you want to know how the current plans apply to you, email me.

Is this against Apple's or GitHub's rules?

No. You're using GitHub's own free macOS minutes to build your own app, on your own repo, under your own Apple Developer account — exactly what they're for. This isn't reselling compute or running anyone else's builds.

What if I get stuck?

Email me. The troubleshooting doc covers the errors this pipeline has actually produced, and if you can't get a build out within 30 days, I'll refund you.
