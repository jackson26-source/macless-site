What you're actually buying

This is the part worth being precise about, because it's the one thing that genuinely differs from every hosted CI service.

The pipeline is a set of files in your own GitHub repository. A workflow file, signing scripts, and docs. When it runs, it runs on GitHub's macOS and Linux runners, billed to your GitHub account — free on public repos, and out of your monthly allowance on private ones. It signs with certificates you generated under your own Apple Developer account, stored as encrypted GitHub secrets in your repo. There is no Macless server between your code and Apple. If macless.dev went dark tomorrow, your builds would still run tonight.

So you own the pipeline whether you're subscribed or not. Cancel the monthly and your builds keep working exactly as they did the day before — same workflow file, same secrets, same green runs.

What the payment covers is the hosted side of it: the setup wizard that installs and configures all of that for you, the certificate and profile expiry monitoring and renewal, the extras in Signing Doctor and Rejection Doctor, and the work of keeping the pipeline current as Apple shifts its signing and submission requirements — which it does, without warning, on its own schedule.

Before you ask

What happens to my pipeline if I cancel the monthly?

Nothing happens to it. The workflow files, signing scripts, and docs are committed in your repository, your credentials are in your repository's encrypted secrets, and the builds run on your GitHub Actions minutes. None of that is checked against a subscription, and none of it phones home. Your next git push after cancelling builds and uploads exactly like the one before it. What you stop getting is the hosted side: the wizard for setting up new repos, certificate and profile expiry monitoring and renewal, the Doctors' extras, and updates when Apple changes something. The free Signing Doctor and Rejection Doctor core checks stay available to you like they do to everyone.

Is Android included, or is that extra?

Included, in both paid plans. iOS and Android are one product now — the same wizard, the same secrets handling, the same expiry monitoring across your iOS certificates and your Android keystore. Android never needed a Mac in the first place; it needed the same signing setup nobody wants to hand-assemble twice.

What does the $19.99 a month actually pay for, if I already own the pipeline?

Three things, honestly stated. First, the wizard — the difference between reading a setup guide and clicking through six steps, including pushing your signing secrets into GitHub for you. Second, monitoring and renewal — a certificate or profile expiring is the single most common way a working pipeline breaks months later, and that watching has to keep happening on a schedule. Third, maintenance — Apple changes its signing rules, its Xcode versions, and its App Store Connect requirements, and the pipeline has to be re-tested and fixed against those changes. The changelog is the public, dated record of that third one, so you can judge it before you pay for it rather than after.

Is the Apple Developer Program fee included?

No, and to be unambiguous about it: Apple charges $99 per year for the Apple Developer Program, you pay that directly to Apple, and it has nothing to do with Macless. You need it to put an app on the App Store or TestFlight at all, regardless of what tool you build with. Macless removes the Mac requirement, not Apple's fee.

Which plan should I pick?

They're the same product. $299 is about fifteen months of the monthly, so if you expect to be shipping this app for longer than that, the one-time is cheaper; if you're not sure the project will outlive the year, the monthly costs you less to find out. Neither one changes what lands in your repo.

Do the free tools have a catch?

No account, no card, no usage cap, no trial clock. Signing Doctor and Rejection Doctor's core checks are public tools and they stay that way — they're the part of this I'd want to exist even if nobody ever bought anything. The paid tier adds saved projects, history, and batch checks on top; it doesn't take anything away from the free version.

Are there build costs on top of this?

Not from me. The builds run on GitHub Actions under your account: public repositories get unlimited free macOS minutes, and private repositories get a monthly allowance before GitHub bills you per minute past it. Either way that's your bill with GitHub, at GitHub's rates, and Macless doesn't mark it up or sit in the middle of it.

Can I start monthly and switch to the one-time later?

They're two separate purchases through Stripe, so nothing switches automatically. Email me before you buy the one-time rather than buying blind, and I'll tell you where you stand.

I already bought the old $99 or $39 one-time. Where does that leave me?

Nothing is taken away from you. What you downloaded is in your repo and keeps running, the same as it did the day you bought it. If you want to know how the new plans apply to you, email me.

Is this against Apple's or GitHub's rules?

No. You're using GitHub's own free macOS minutes to build your own app, on your own repo, under your own Apple Developer account — exactly what they're for. This isn't reselling compute or running anyone else's builds.

What if I get stuck?

Email me. The troubleshooting doc covers the errors this pipeline has actually produced, and if you can't get a build out within 30 days, I'll refund you.
