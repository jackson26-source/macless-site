Can Draftbit publish your app to the App Store and Google Play?

Yes, genuinely, and Draftbit earned that. Of every platform in this comparison it has the most complete publishing process, confirmed in its own documentation rather than its marketing.

Jackson · October 2026

What Draftbit actually does, credit where it's due

On its Standard plan or higher, Draftbit builds your app, handles the security files Apple and Google require, and uploads the result to both stores under your own developer accounts.

When that works there's genuinely less for us to add. We won't pretend otherwise.

What it still leaves you

Google requires the very first Android upload by hand, no matter what Draftbit automates afterward. If you've never opened the Google Play Console, that's a wall on day one.

The listing is yours too: the description, the screenshots, the privacy policy, the category. Draftbit builds the app, it doesn't write your store page.

And "Draftbit handled the security files" doesn't mean anyone checked that your login return addresses, your backend's security requirements, or your forms behave correctly in the finished app rather than the preview.

We do that first upload if it's blocking you, write the listing, and test the finished app against your real backend. Macless is an all in one, built, debugged and shipped, so none of those leftover pieces land back on you.

Apple's rejection emails are still yours

Draftbit uploads. It doesn't argue with Apple on your behalf, decode a vague rejection, or resubmit.

We do.

The features you haven't thought to ask for

A notification that opens the exact screen it's about. A badge on your app icon. An Apple Wallet pass. Face ID sign-in. A real camera scanner. The share sheet. Background location for drivers and field work.

Subscriptions as well, since Apple requires its own payment system. It's likely we can build that in depending on your app.

We can build the app itself, not just ship it

This is the real pitch for Draftbit customers, because publishing isn't your problem. Building past what Draftbit builds is.

The staff admin side separated from the customer side. A partner app and a dispatch app on the same data, each with its own login and listing. Data exports. Server-side logic. Post-launch bug fixes when real users find what testing didn't.

Summary

Draftbit's publishing is a real strength and it stops at publishing.

Everything after, and everything bigger than what Draftbit can build, is ours.
