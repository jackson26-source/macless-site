Fix "Provisioning profile doesn't include signing certificate"

This one's confusing because the profile genuinely isn't expired and genuinely isn't misspelled — it's just pinned to a certificate that no longer exists.

Jackson · August 2026

The exact error looks something like this:

error: Provisioning profile "YourApp App Store" doesn't include
signing certificate "Apple Distribution: Your Name (XXXXXXXXXX)".

It's a confusing one the first time you hit it, because everything looks fine: the profile shows as Active in the Apple Developer portal, the bundle ID matches, the team ID matches. The thing that's actually wrong isn't visible from any of the fields you'd normally check.

What's actually happening

A provisioning profile doesn't just declare an App ID and a team — it's baked at generation time against one or more specific certificates, referenced by their exact public key. It's not "any valid Distribution certificate for this team," it's "these particular certificates, by fingerprint." If the certificate your CI job (or Xcode) is currently using isn't one of those exact certificates, this error fires — even though, from every other angle, the profile looks completely valid.

This almost always happens in one specific sequence: a Distribution certificate expires or gets revoked, someone generates a fresh one to replace it, and the provisioning profile — which was pinned to the old certificate — never gets regenerated to match. The profile doesn't expire when this happens. It just quietly stops working with your current certificate.

How to confirm this is actually what's wrong

Before regenerating anything, check the fingerprint match directly rather than guessing. Two ways to do it:

Manually, with openssl: extract the certificates embedded in the profile and compare their fingerprints against your current .p12:

$ security cms -D -i YourApp.mobileprovision > profile.plist
$ /usr/libexec/PlistBuddy -c "Print DeveloperCertificates:0" profile.plist \
    | openssl x509 -inform DER -noout -fingerprint

$ openssl pkcs12 -in dist.p12 -nokeys -passin pass:yourpassword \
    | openssl x509 -noout -fingerprint

If those two fingerprints don't match, that's the whole problem confirmed in two commands.

Or skip the manual steps — this is exactly the check Signing Doctor, a free tool I put together, automates. Point it at your profile and certificate and it tells you directly whether they match, instead of you extracting and comparing fingerprints by hand:

$ ./signing-doctor.sh --profile YourApp.mobileprovision --cert dist.p12
FAIL  Your certificate's fingerprint does NOT match any certificate
      embedded in this profile.

The fix

Once confirmed, the fix is straightforward: in the Apple Developer portal, open the provisioning profile and regenerate it. Regenerating re-links it to whichever Distribution certificate is currently active for your team. Download the new profile, re-encode it, and update wherever your CI stores it (a GitHub secret, in most setups) — the old one can be safely deleted once the new one's working.

If you manage certificates for a team, this is the exact failure mode that "automatically manage signing" in Xcode's GUI papers over — it silently regenerates mismatched profiles for you. CI has no equivalent, so a certificate rotation that Xcode would have absorbed invisibly becomes a hard failure in a headless pipeline. Worth regenerating the relevant profiles as a matter of course any time a Distribution certificate is rotated, rather than waiting for a build to fail first.
