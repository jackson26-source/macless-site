Can Bubble publish your app to the App Store and Google Play?

Yes, through two routes. Bubble's Native Mobile App Editor builds iPhone and Android apps from Mobile views, and its docs describe publishing them to both stores. A Bubble web app goes through a third-party wrapper instead, which Bubble's docs acknowledge.

Jackson · October 2026, updated November 2026

Route one: Bubble's native mobile editor

If you build with Mobile views, Bubble generates the app for you. Its FAQ says that once you have an active Apple Developer account and have uploaded the certificate and provisioning files through Bubble's interface, Bubble handles the rest of the build process. After that, the FAQ says it takes about 45 minutes to generate the build, upload it to TestFlight and submit it for review.

You still generate three things yourself from Apple: a .p8 key (the App Store Connect API key), the app's bundle identifier and the Apple Developer Team ID. The FAQ names mistakes in these three as where people most often get stuck.

Android works too. Bubble builds an Android App Bundle (AAB) file that you upload to Google Play. Bubble says it supports publishing to Google Play only, not other Android stores such as Amazon.

Route two: wrapping a Bubble web app

Bubble's own docs say there are third-party and custom code options for publishing a web-built Bubble app to the stores. They work by wrapping the app in a native container, which the docs compare to a full-screen browser. The docs say these solutions power many successful Bubble apps in the stores, and that they may not match the performance or device integration of a fully native app. They also depend on third-party services, plugins or custom code.

What does Bubble leave to you?

The store accounts. Apple's developer program costs $99 a year and Google Play's registration is a one-time $25, per Bubble's docs. You also supply the icons, descriptions, screenshots and a public privacy policy.

Review is yours. The docs put Apple's wait at typically one to three days, longer if revisions are needed, and the FAQ lists the common rejection reasons: privacy policy issues, unsupported content, and incomplete functionality such as submitting a shell without full content.

What needs a new submission?

The docs separate two kinds of changes. Edits to the UI or logic inside Bubble don't need resubmission, and over-the-air updates can reach users without a new build. Native changes such as the splash screen, icons or plugin settings need a new build and another submission. Bubble's docs add that most updates still require a new build file.

What can't the native editor do?

The docs have an entry explaining why the repeating group isn't available in native mobile apps, and another on why some web elements aren't available. Check that list against your app before you commit to rebuilding it as a mobile app.
