"Unable to process request - PLA Update available"

Nothing in your project changed. Apple published a new agreement, and only one specific person can accept it.

Jackson · September 2026

Your build was fine yesterday. Today Xcode or your CI job stops with:

Unable to process request - PLA Update available

Read it as what it is: PLA means Program License Agreement. Apple has published a new version of the agreement your developer account operates under, and until someone accepts it, Apple's developer APIs refuse to do useful work — fetching profiles, creating certificates, uploading builds.

Nothing is wrong with your certificates, your profiles, your entitlements, or your project. This is Apple's side, waiting on a signature.

Only the Account Holder can accept it

This is the part that turns a two-minute fix into a two-day one. Admins can't accept it. Developers can't accept it. Only the Account Holder — the single Apple ID that owns the membership — can. If that's a founder who's on holiday, or a former colleague, or an account nobody has logged into for a year, your releases are blocked until you reach them.

Worth knowing before you need it: find out who your Account Holder is now, while nothing is broken.

Accepting it

  1. Sign in to developer.apple.com/account as the Account Holder.
  2. A banner or a "Review Agreement" prompt appears at the top. Accept it.
  3. If you sell paid apps or use in-app purchase, also check App Store Connect → Business for a separate Paid Applications agreement waiting on the same person.
  4. Re-run the build. No code change, no clean, no certificate regeneration.

It often shows up wearing a disguise. Because the agreement block breaks profile and certificate lookups, the error you actually see can be something further downstream — "No signing certificate 'iOS Distribution' found" is a common one. If a signing error appears suddenly with no change on your side, check for a pending agreement before you start regenerating anything. Regenerating certificates you didn't need to regenerate is how a bad afternoon becomes a bad week.

Why CI makes it worse

Locally, you're signed in, and Xcode often surfaces the prompt where you'll see it. A CI runner is signed in as nobody — it has an API key and no way to be shown a dialog. So the pipeline just fails, and the message it prints doesn't contain the words "go accept an agreement." Apple publishes these periodically and without warning, which is why a pipeline that hasn't changed in months can break on a Tuesday.

There's no way to pre-empt it from inside a build. The only real mitigation is knowing what the message means, so the fix takes minutes instead of an afternoon spent suspecting your own setup.
